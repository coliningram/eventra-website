How to Watch a Formula 1 Grand Prix in 2026: Tickets, Hospitality and What It Costs

Published [date] 6 minute read

Most people asking about Formula 1 do not actually know what they are buying. They know they want to be at a race. What they do not know is that "going to a Grand Prix" can mean a grandstand seat, a hospitality lounge, the Paddock Club, or a place on a superyacht in the Monaco harbour, at prices that run from a few hundred euros to well into five figures for the same weekend.

This is the honest version. What each option really is, who it suits, which races cost the most and why, and when you actually need to book. No hype. If you take one thing from it, take this. The ticket is the easy part. The trip around it is what decides whether the weekend is good or unforgettable, and it is the part that sells out first.

The ways to actually be there

General admission. A ground pass, no fixed seat. The cheapest way in, and it works well at circuits with natural elevation like Spa or Zandvoort where a grass bank can beat a low grandstand. Suits a fit, easygoing fan. Not for anyone entertaining guests.

Grandstand. A reserved seat in a specific stand, held all weekend. Pick your view, the start-finish straight, a heavy braking zone, a famous corner. This is the sensible default for most serious fans.

Official F1 Experiences packages. The sport's own branded package: a grandstand seat plus a guided track tour, a pit-lane walk, driver and personality appearances, and at some races a grid walk or a helicopter flight over the circuit. A real step up from a bare ticket without the full hospitality price.

Formula 1 Paddock Club. The premier hospitality tier, trackside above the pit lane. Gourmet dining, open bar, pit-lane walks, and a view straight down onto the cars being worked on. This is the one most people mean when they say "hospitality," and it is priced to match.

Private suites, lounges and yacht hospitality. Named suites and lounges around the circuit, and at the two races on the water, Monaco and Abu Dhabi, a place on a trackside superyacht. Champagne, live cooking, a DJ, and the cars a few metres away. This is the top of the experience, and at Monaco it is the whole point.

The hotel-inclusive weekend. Ticket or hospitality bundled with hotel, transfers and the logistics handled end to end. At the harder races this is not a luxury, it is the only version that actually works. It is what we do, and more on why below.

Which races cost what

Not all Grands Prix are priced the same, and it is not random. Scarcity of the circuit, cost of the destination, and where the race sits in the season all drive it. The numbers back up the reputations, mostly.

Las Vegas is the most expensive to get into, a grandstand starts around €1,400, because racing down the Strip at night is a destination event priced like one. Singapore, the original night race, is next. At the other end, Spa, Qatar and the Red Bull Ring are the most reachable, with real three-day grandstands from around €300 to €500. Monaco sits outside the pattern entirely. There is no cheap way in, because it is sold as hospitality and terraces rather than grandstands. And the hospitality peaks are their own league. Mexico's Paddock Club and Monza's Ferrari GP Club both run into five figures.

The premium and signature experiences

Above the grandstands is where a Grand Prix becomes something else, and what is open varies by race. The standouts we can arrange:

  • Monaco, on the water. A place on a 44-metre trackside superyacht in the harbour, open bar, live cooking, the cars a few metres away, from ~€3,150 for Saturday. Nothing else in the sport compares, and it is the reason people go to Monaco rather than watch it.
  • Formula 1 Paddock Club, the official flagship tier, currently available at the Hungarian (from ~€7,100), Dutch (from ~€7,950), Azerbaijan (from ~€5,750) and Mexico City (from ~€11,500) Grands Prix. At every other race we source it to order.
  • Monza and the Ferrari GP Club, inside the red wall of the tifosi, from ~€9,600.
  • Las Vegas SkyBox above the Strip, from ~€7,600.
  • Abu Dhabi, also on the water, a superyacht hospitality berth at Yas Marina from ~€900.
  • Qatar Champions Club with a grid walk, from ~€4,900.

Named suites and lounges sit between grandstand and Paddock Club at most races, the National Gallery terrace at Singapore, the W Hotel track-view suites at Abu Dhabi, Club 91 at Madrid, the Schumacher Lounge at Monza, and more. Tell us the race and we will lay out exactly what is open.

When should you book?

Earlier than you think, and here is the honest reason.

Tickets are rarely the real constraint. Hotels are. At Monaco, Las Vegas, Abu Dhabi and Singapore, the good hotels fill twelve to eighteen months out, sell only on multi-night minimums over the race weekend, and price those nights at a heavy premium. By the time general ticket sales open, the accommodation that makes the trip workable is often already gone.

So if a specific race is on your list, you are really booking against hotel availability, not ticket availability. That is the single most useful thing to understand about planning a Grand Prix, and it is the reason we take the whole weekend on rather than just selling a seat.

Can you build a Grand Prix weekend into a bigger trip?

Yes, and for anyone flying a long way it is the sensible way to do it. The race is a few hours. Crossing the world for a single afternoon is a lot of travel for one result.

Every circuit sits somewhere worth staying for. Spa pairs with the Ardennes or a few days in Amsterdam, Monza with Milan and the Italian lakes, Monaco with the Riviera, Singapore with the islands, Las Vegas with the canyons of the American West, Abu Dhabi with the wider Emirates. We work with trusted destination partners on the ground across Europe and beyond, so the days either side of the race are built by people who know their own patch rather than booked blind from a distance. Tell us where you are flying from and what you want out of the trip, and we build the whole thing around the race.

What we would tell you honestly

Three things a ticket reseller will not.

You do not need the Paddock Club at every race. At a circuit like Spa, a good grandstand on the right corner beats a hospitality suite for the pure spectacle of it. Save the premium option for the race where it matters to you.

The cheapest ticket is a false economy if you are flying in. Once you have paid for long-haul flights and a weekend of race-inflated hotel rates, saving a little on the seat to sit somewhere you cannot see makes no sense. Get the part you travelled for right.

And the trip is the thing, not the ticket. The race is a few hours. The weekend around it, where you stay, how you get in and out, what else you see, is what makes it. That is the part worth handing to someone who does it for a living.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a ticket, hospitality and the Paddock Club?

A ticket is a seat or a ground pass, and you arrange everything else yourself. Hospitality adds a premium space, food and drink and a better view, sold per person. The Formula 1 Paddock Club is the sport's official top tier, trackside above the pit lane with dining, open bar and pit walks. Private suites, lounges and yacht berths are exclusive spaces for your own group.

What is the difference between general admission and a grandstand?

General admission is a ground pass with no fixed seat, so you move around the standing and viewing areas and find your own spot. A grandstand is a reserved seat in a specific stand that you keep all weekend, with a known view of a particular corner or straight. General admission is cheaper and works well at circuits with natural elevation like Spa. A grandstand is the safer choice if you want a guaranteed view, or you are bringing people who would rather not stand all day.

What does the Formula 1 Paddock Club include?

The Paddock Club sits trackside above the pit lane and includes gourmet dining, an open bar through the day, and a guided pit-lane walk, with a paddock tour and appearances from drivers or motorsport figures at most races. You watch from a terrace directly over the garages. It is the sport's official premium hospitality, all-inclusive on food and drink, and the exact programme varies by circuit.

Is F1 hospitality worth it?

It depends on the race and what you want from the weekend. At a great circuit like Spa, a well-placed grandstand can beat hospitality for the pure racing. Where hospitality earns its money is at the destination races, and when you are entertaining guests or travelling a long way, the comfort, the catering and the view over the pits change the day. Our honest advice is to spend on hospitality at the one or two races where it matters most to you, and take a good grandstand elsewhere.

How much does the F1 Paddock Club cost?

It varies widely by race. In our current inventory the Paddock Club runs from around €5,750 for a weekend at the Azerbaijan Grand Prix up past €11,500 at Mexico City, where demand is highest, with most flagship races in between. We quote the exact package, and source the Paddock Club at races where it is not on open sale, on enquiry.

What is the dress code for F1 hospitality?

It varies by tier. Grandstands and general admission have no dress code, so wear what is comfortable for a long day outdoors. Hospitality lounges and the Paddock Club are generally smart casual, and some premium suites and yacht events lean dressier. We confirm the dress code for your specific package when you book.

What is the best F1 race to attend?

It depends what you want. For pure racing on a proper circuit, Spa-Francorchamps is hard to beat. For spectacle and prestige, nothing touches Monaco. For a night out with the race as the centrepiece, Las Vegas and Singapore both run after dark. Monza brings the loudest crowd in the sport. We can talk you through the trade-offs for your dates.

What is the most expensive F1 race to attend?

Las Vegas has the highest entry price of any race, with grandstands from around €1,400, followed by Singapore. For hospitality, Mexico City's Paddock Club and Monza's Ferrari GP Club are the dearest, both into five figures. Monaco is the most expensive to do properly, because it is sold as hospitality and yacht berths rather than cheap seats.

Can you watch the Monaco Grand Prix from a yacht?

Yes. We can arrange places on a 44-metre trackside superyacht in the Monaco harbour, with open bar, live cooking and the cars metres away, from around €3,150. It is the definitive way to experience Monaco.

When do F1 tickets go on sale?

Official sales open on a rolling basis per race through the season. Tickets are rarely the bottleneck though. The hotels are, and the best of them are booked more than a year ahead for the marquee races.

Do you offer corporate F1 hospitality and packages?

Yes. Private suites, trackside lounges, corporate boxes and the harbour yachts all take groups, and we arrange branded hosting for corporate guests, from a handful of clients to a full suite. Tell us the race and the numbers and we will put options in front of you.

Can you arrange F1 hotels and travel packages?

Yes, and at the harder races that is the whole point. We build the full weekend, hospitality or grandstand, hotel, transfers and any extension either side, as one trip rather than a ticket you then have to build a holiday around yourself.

Which races can you book for 2027?

We can book across the 2027 calendar, including Monaco, the Chinese, Japanese, Miami, Canadian and Austrian Grands Prix. If the race you want is not listed, ask, and we will source it.
